## Paragraphs

- Two to four sentences each. A one-sentence paragraph is allowed at most twice per article, for emphasis.
- Never more than about 90 words in a paragraph.
- No two consecutive paragraphs should open with the same word.
- Do not repeat a sentence, or a near-copy of one, anywhere in the article.

## Headings

- The article title is the only H1. Section headings are H2. Use H3 only inside long Deep Dive or Playbook sections, and never more than three per section.
- Headings are short (two to eight words), plain and informative. "The numbers" is better than "Diving into the data".
- Keep the category's required sections in their prescribed order. A writer may make a heading more specific ("Why it matters for Indian lenders") but must not drop or merge required sections.
- No question headings unless the category asks for one (Decisions: "The question").

## Lists

- Use a bulleted list when there are three or more parallel items (options, steps, risks, metrics). Two items belong in a sentence.
- Bullets are parallel in grammar and roughly equal in length. Each bullet is one idea, ideally under 25 words.
- Use a numbered list only for sequential steps (Playbook: "The steps").
- Never put an entire section in bullets; introduce a list with a sentence that says why it matters.

## Comparisons

- When comparing options, companies or periods across two or more attributes, a small table is clearer than prose. Keep tables to at most four columns and six rows.

## Opening move

- The first sentence carries information: an actor, an action and, where available, a number or date.
- No throat-clearing. Do not open with the state of the world, a dictionary definition, a rhetorical question or "In recent years".
- By the end of the first paragraph, the reader knows what happened and why they should keep reading.

## Closing move

- End on something the reader can act on: a decision to revisit, a metric to watch, a test to apply or the condition under which the conclusion flips.
- Do not summarise the article. Do not start the final paragraph with "In conclusion", "Ultimately" or "Overall".

## Pull quotes

- Optional, at most one per 600 words.
- A pull quote must be either an exact quotation from a source (with attribution in the body text) or a sentence that already appears in the article. Never write a new claim inside a pull quote.
- Keep it under 30 words.

## Attribution

- Attribute claims in-line, in plain words: "according to the company's filing", "Reuters reported", "the chief executive told analysts".
- Every number is attributable to a source in the research pack. If a figure is the company's own projection, say so.

## Typography

- Use the tenant's spelling convention consistently (US or UK English).
- Use en dashes for ranges (2024–2026) and em dashes sparingly for asides.
- Spell out numbers one to nine except for units, money and percentages; use numerals from 10 upward.
- Percentages as "12%"; currencies with symbol or ISO code and scale ("$6.4bn", "₹1,200 crore", "EUR 300m") exactly as the source gives them.

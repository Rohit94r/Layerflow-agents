## Purpose

A Deep Dive explains how a company actually works and what other leaders can learn from it: how it makes money, what it is betting on, what the numbers say, what protects it and what could break it. It is the most evidence-heavy format and the one most likely to be wrong if the writer reaches beyond the sources.

- **Route:** `/deep_dive`
- **Target length:** 1,400–2,000 words
- **Primary sources:** annual and quarterly filings (10-K, 10-Q, annual reports), investor presentations, earnings-call transcripts, regulatory disclosures, reputable business reporting. A Deep Dive with a number should rest on at least three independent sources, including a primary one where possible.

## Required sections (in this order)

| Key | Default heading | Length | What it must do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `snapshot` | Snapshot | 120–180 words | Who the company is, its scale and why it is worth studying now. Three or four facts, all sourced. |
| `business_model` | The business model | 180–260 words | What it sells, to whom, and through which channels. Who the real customer is when that differs from the user. |
| `how_it_makes_money` | How it makes money | 180–260 words | Revenue streams, mix, pricing model and the economics of a unit (customer, order, contract) where the sources allow. |
| `strategic_moves` | Strategic moves | 200–300 words | The two to four most important recent decisions — acquisitions, market entries, investments, exits — with dates and stated rationale. |
| `the_numbers` | The numbers | 150–250 words | The figures that matter, with period and comparison. A small table is preferred. Every figure from the research pack, exactly as the source gives it. |
| `moat` | The moat | 150–230 words | What protects the business — and the evidence for it. Name the mechanism (switching costs, scale, network, regulation, brand, data), not just the word "moat". |
| `risks` | The risks | 150–230 words | Three to five risks as bullets: competitive, regulatory, execution, concentration, financial. Each with the signal to watch. |
| `lessons` | Lessons for leaders | 150–230 words | What a leader in a different company or sector should take from this, and the condition under which the lesson does not transfer. |

## Do

- Separate reported results from management guidance and from analyst estimates.
- Name the period for every number (FY2025, Q2 2026, trailing twelve months).
- Use primary sources for financial figures wherever the pack contains them.
- Be as specific about weaknesses as about strengths.

## Don't

- Don't compute new ratios, growth rates or valuations that are not in the sources.
- Don't use remembered figures from training data, however confident.
- Don't write a hagiography; the risks section must be real.
- Don't exceed 2,000 words; tighten the business model sections first.

## Example opening move (illustrative)

"Fabrikam Robotics sells fewer robots than any of its three main rivals and makes more money from each one — a gap that comes almost entirely from what happens after installation."

## Example closing move (illustrative)

"The transferable lesson is not 'sell services'. It is that Fabrikam priced its installed base before it priced its hardware. Leaders who sell durable equipment should ask whether their service revenue is designed or merely accumulated."

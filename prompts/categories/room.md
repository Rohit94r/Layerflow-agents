## Purpose

A Room piece puts the reader inside a real decision: the people, the pressure, the options on the table and the moment the choice was made. It is built **only** from material supplied to us — an interview transcript, recorded conversation, earnings-call transcript or first-person account. It is never imagined.

- **Route:** `/room`
- **Target length:** 1,000–1,500 words
- **Primary sources:** supplied interview transcripts, recorded conversations, earnings-call or conference transcripts, first-person accounts provided to TEN. News reports may add context but never invent scenes.

## Hard rule

If the research pack does not contain transcript, interview or first-person material, this category must not be written. Report insufficient evidence instead. No composite characters, no invented dialogue, no reconstructed scenes beyond what the material states.

## Required sections (in this order)

| Key | Default heading | Length | What it must do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `setting` | The setting | 150–220 words | Who was in the room, what the business was facing, and the stakes — all from the material. Dates and numbers only where the source gives them. |
| `the_decision` | The decision | 170–250 words | The choice that had to be made, framed as the participants framed it. What were the options? What was the deadline or forcing event? |
| `the_debate` | The debate | 250–380 words | The arguments for and against, attributed to the people who made them, using exact quotations where the transcript provides them. Show disagreement honestly. |
| `turning_point` | The turning point | 170–260 words | The fact, argument or event that settled the matter, in the participants' own words where possible. |
| `what_it_reveals` | What it reveals | 170–260 words | The lesson for other leaders: the decision pattern, the mistake avoided or made, and when the same logic would not apply. |

## Do

- Quote exactly. Every quotation must appear verbatim in the supplied material, attributed to its speaker.
- Mark paraphrase as paraphrase ("she said, in effect, that…").
- Keep the narrative tight: one decision, not a company history.
- Make "What it reveals" transferable to the reader's own situation.

## Don't

- Don't invent dialogue, gestures, settings or inner thoughts.
- Don't merge different meetings into one scene unless the source does.
- Don't name people who are not named in the material.
- Don't moralise about the outcome; analyse it.

## Example opening move (illustrative)

"The question in front of Contoso Bank's executive committee that Tuesday was not whether to close the branch network, but how fast — and the chief operating officer, by her own account, wanted the answer to be 'slower than anyone in this room thinks.'"

## Example closing move (illustrative)

"The lesson is not that slower is safer. It is that the committee agreed in advance which signal would make them speed up — and wrote it down before anyone had a stake in being right."

## Purpose

A Brief tells a senior leader, in under three minutes, what just happened in a deal, investment, funding round, earnings result, leadership change or regulatory move — and why it should change how they think. It is news with judgment, not news with adjectives.

- **Route:** `/brief`
- **Target length:** 350–550 words
- **Primary sources:** wire and business news reports, company press releases, regulatory filings (8-K, stock-exchange disclosures), investor presentations.

## Required sections (in this order)

| Key | Default heading | Length | What it must do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `lede` | Lede (no heading shown) | ≤40 words | One or two sentences: who did what, how big, when. The single most important number belongs here if there is one. |
| `what_happened` | What happened | 80–130 words | The facts in order of importance: parties, terms, timing, conditions, who said what (with attribution). No interpretation yet. |
| `the_numbers` | The numbers | 60–120 words | Three to five figures that matter, each with unit, period and comparison where the source gives one. A short bulleted list is preferred. Only numbers that appear in the research pack. |
| `why_it_matters` | Why it matters | 100–160 words | The executive reading: what changes for competitors, customers, suppliers or capital markets. Name the second-order effect. This is where the piece earns its place. |
| `what_to_watch` | What to watch | 50–100 words | Two or three concrete, observable signals — a closing date, a regulatory decision, the next earnings call, a metric — that will confirm or refute the reading. |

## Do

- Put the decisive fact in the first sentence.
- Distinguish announced, committed and completed. A signed deal subject to approval is not a closed deal.
- Attribute every figure and every forward-looking statement.
- Keep "Why it matters" specific to a type of reader: a rival chief executive, a supplier, a lender, an investor.

## Don't

- Don't pad with company history. One clause of context is enough.
- Don't speculate about valuation, motives or outcomes the sources do not support.
- Don't copy press-release language ("strategic", "transformative", "world-leading").
- Don't exceed 550 words. If it needs more, it is a Deep Dive.

## Example opening move (illustrative)

"Northwind Logistics will buy rival Fabrikam Freight for $1.2bn in cash, its largest acquisition, giving it a third of the regional cold-chain market if regulators agree."

## Example closing move (illustrative)

"Watch the competition authority's first-phase decision, due within 30 working days. If it opens an in-depth review, the financing — which expires in March — becomes the story."

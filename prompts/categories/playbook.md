## Purpose

A Playbook gives a senior leader a method they can use: a framework that solves a recurring problem, the steps to apply it, an example of it working, and an honest account of when it fails. It is practical, testable and grounded in practitioner evidence.

- **Route:** `/playbook`
- **Target length:** 1,000–1,400 words
- **Primary sources:** practitioner writing, management research, case studies, company disclosures describing how a practice was implemented, operator interviews.

## Required sections (in this order)

| Key | Default heading | Length | What it must do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `problem` | The problem | 120–180 words | The recurring problem, who has it, and what it costs when handled badly. One sourced example or number makes it real. |
| `framework` | The framework | 170–250 words | The model in one sentence, then its components. Give it a plain, descriptive name; no acronyms for their own sake. A small table or diagram-in-words helps. |
| `steps` | The steps | 220–320 words | A numbered list of four to seven steps. Each step: what to do, who owns it, and what "done" looks like. |
| `worked_example` | A worked example | 180–260 words | One real, sourced case showing the framework (or a close equivalent) in practice, including what it changed. If the sources only support a partial example, say so. |
| `failure_modes` | Failure modes | 130–200 words | Three to five ways the framework goes wrong in practice, as bullets, each with the early warning sign. |
| `when_not_to_use` | When not to use it | 90–150 words | The conditions under which a different approach is better. This section is what makes the playbook credible. |

## Do

- Make each step executable: a verb, an owner, an output.
- Use sourced evidence for any claim that the method works.
- Say what the framework costs to run — time, people, political capital.
- Keep the vocabulary operational, not academic.

## Don't

- Don't present the framework as universal.
- Don't invent a worked example or a company that "used this method". If no real example exists in the sources, describe the example as hypothetical and carry no numbers in it.
- Don't produce more than seven steps.
- Don't brand the framework with a catchy acronym unless the source uses one.

## Example opening move (illustrative)

"Most pricing changes fail not because the new price is wrong but because nobody owns the conversation with the 20 customers who will push back first."

## Example closing move (illustrative)

"Run the first cycle on one product line, with one owner, for one quarter. If the renewal rate on repriced accounts holds within two points of the old rate, extend it. If not, the problem is the value story, not the price."
